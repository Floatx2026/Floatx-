The Case for a Unicorn Fund

Why late-stage private equity is the right product to build — and why now is the right time

LP Demand
Unmet allocator demand — and it's growing

Family offices, endowments, and institutional allocators are lifting private-market allocations to 20–30% of portfolio. Most can't access top-tier late-stage deals directly — they lack the relationships and can't meet minimum ticket sizes.

A fund closes that access gap at scale.
Differentiation
Quality supply is the bottleneck

Clean structure + lean fees + genuine deal access equals a product that stands out. In a category where credible vehicles are rare, a well-run fund is differentiated rather than commoditised.

Scarcity of supply = pricing power + LP loyalty.
Timing
The category window is open now

Allocators are moving into private markets in this cycle. Peer vehicles are launching. First-mover positioning with the right distribution advantage compounds — the window is real but not indefinite.

Right category, right network, right moment.
The Moat

Advantage

Unicorn Fund vs PPP — Key Points

Why a unicorn fund addresses the specific weaknesses that constrained the existing pre-IPO product

✓ What a unicorn fund fixes
Addresses PPP's structural weaknesses
  • ›Breaks exit dependence — secondary markets allow pricing and exit without needing an IPO window
  • ›Global scope removes concentration in Australia's thin IPO pipeline
  • ›Larger opportunity set — 1,300+ global unicorns vs. 30–45 domestic names
  • ›Evergreen structure option avoids the forced-exit trap that hurt closed-end PPP vintages
  • ›Durable structural shift — companies staying private far longer, capturing growth pre-listing
  • ›Deep liquidity pool — much larger companies create sustained secondary-market demand
◐ What PPP actually showed
Structure was the constraint — not the asset class
  • ›Strong start, then stalled — 31.89% year one, faded to flat/negative once the IPO window closed post-2022
  • ›Problem was structural — exit dependence + domestic concentration, not the asset class itself
  • ›PPP+ response — Perennial's own answer was evergreen + valuation discipline, not a pivot to unicorns
A unicorn fund is the next logical evolution of PPP+ — same discipline, broader opportunity set, better liquidity story.
★ Bottom Line
Structure & discipline,
not the label

A unicorn fund fixes the specific things that broke PPP — exit dependence and geographic concentration.

The edge comes from how it's structured and how it's run — not the marketing label.

Same underwriting discipline as PPP+. Broader opportunity set. Better liquidity story.

Private and Confidential 08 / 12

SPV share class structures

Four routes to the same position — different risk, rights and cost profiles

Layer 1 · Top-tier share-holding vehicle

Directly holds the unicorn shares

  • Directly holds the unicorn shares
  • Appears on the cap table as the registered holder
  • Has whatever voting, information and consent rights attach to those shares
  • Signs the SPA, deals with ROFR, gets the company's transfer consent
  • Is the single legal shareholder from the unicorn's perspective
Fees & economics

Direct ownership, direct rights

Access fee 8% – 12%
  • On issuer share register as holder of record
  • Requires ROFR process and transfer approval
  • Net to position: ~88% – 92%
  • GP lock-up: issuer standard (180 days)
Level 1.5 · Intermediary

Forward contract tracking the underlying shares

No share transfer. The existing holder enters a participation interest or forward contract with the SPV. Economic return tracks shares 1:1.

Access fee 13%+
Key features

Speed over registration

  • Holder remains on cap table — no transfer
  • Bypasses ROFR — no issuer approval needed
  • Net to position: ~87%
  • GP lock-up: issuer standard (180 days)
L2 + side letter · Hybrid

L2 access with L1-equivalent rights

Investor subscribes at L2 but a side letter grants L1-equivalent rights — cap table visibility and transfer rights — bridging the two structures.

Fee profile Included in L1 fee
Key features

The best of both

  • Side letter grants L1-level economic rights
  • Requires separate issuer acknowledgement
  • Bridges L2 access with L1 protections
  • Performance fee: 10%–20% above hurdle
Layer 2 · Lower-tier feeder

Feeder hanging off L1 — economics only

  • Holds an interest in L1, not in the unicorn
  • Passes through economics only — dividends, sale proceeds, liquidity events
  • No voting rights in the unicorn — voting stays at L1
  • This is where the downstream investors subscribe
  • Can be one of several L2s feeding into the same L1
Fees & economics

Pooled access, smaller tickets

Management fee 1% – 2% p.a.
  • May carry additional management fee / carry
  • Used for smaller tickets or pooled access
  • Performance fee: 10%–20% above hurdle on liquidation
Layer 3 · Multi-layer wrapper

Fund of funds of funds

An L3 structure wraps the position in three or more entities: the issuer's L1 SPV, an intermediate feeder, and a further fund-of-funds that ultimately holds the LP interest. Most heavily intermediated route.

Layered fees Compounded
Key features

Maximum intermediation

  • 3+ entities between investor and cap table
  • Multiple management fees stack across layers
  • No visibility to underlying holder of record
  • Used where direct access is not available
